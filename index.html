          Framework Architecture

          Strategy Overview

          01
          Foundational Principle

          What is a Trading Strategy, and Why Do Traders Use One?

          A trading strategy is an immutable, predefined set of objective rules governing how a trader discovers market opportunities, executes entry triggers, sizes risk, and exits profitable or losing positions.

          Executing a Defined Strategy
          • Statistical Edge: Positive mathematical expectancy proven across 100+ sample trades.
          • Emotional Immunity: Eliminates fear, greed, FOMO, and revenge trading.
          • Capital Preservation: Sized mathematically to withstand consecutive inevitable losing streaks.
          • Diagnosable & Measurable: Allows precise auditing in your trade journal to optimize performance.
          Taking Random / Impulsive Trades
          • Negative Expectancy: Eroded by bid-ask spread, transaction fees, and impulsive slippage.
          • Emotional Rollercoaster: Chasing green candles and panic-selling pullbacks.
          • Variable Sizing: Bet sizes change emotionally, causing one large loss to wipe out 10 wins.
          • Account Ruin: Unpredictable outcomes leading to eventual capital liquidation.
          Key Takeaway: A strategy converts trading from emotional gambling into an executable statistical business.
          Next: Define Operating Environment
          02
          Market Regime & Environment

          What Market Conditions Does the Strategy Work Best In?

          No strategy works in all market environments. A trader must define the exact market regime, asset class, liquidity window, and timeframe where the strategy possesses a verified statistical advantage.

          1. Asset Class Market & Instruments

          Equities (Cash Stocks), Index Derivatives (F&O), Forex Pairs (Majors/Crosses), or Crypto.

          2. Timeframe Resolution Alignment

          Higher Timeframe (Daily/4H/1H for structural trend) + Lower Timeframe (15m/5m/1m for trigger execution).

          3. Session Liquidity Active Trading Window

          London Open, New York Overlap, Asian Range, or Indian Market Opening Rush (09:15 – 10:30 IST).

          4. Market State Volatility & Trend

          Strong Trending (expansion), Mean-Reverting (rangebound), High Volatility (ATR spikes), or Low Volatility (compression).

          Key Takeaway: Never trade a trend-following system in low-volume sideways chop, or a range-fade system in violent macro breakouts.
          Next: Identify the Pattern
          03
          Setup Identification

          What Setup or Signal Identifies a Potential Trade?

          The setup is the preliminary structural anomaly or technical pattern indicating that an imbalance of supply and demand has occurred and a trade idea is formulating.

          Pattern A Liquidity Sweep (Turtle Soup)

          Price punches past a key high/low (PDH/PDL or equal highs) to absorb retail stops and immediately rejects back inside the range.

          Pattern B Key Level Compression & Breakout

          Multi-touch horizontal level with rising swing lows compressing beneath resistance, followed by high-volume range expansion.

          Pattern C Value Area Pullback (VWAP / EMA)

          In a confirmed trend, price pulls back to the dynamic institutional average (VWAP / 20 EMA) on declining corrective volume.

          Key Takeaway: If you cannot describe your setup in one clear, concise sentence, you do not have a defined setup.
          Next: Trigger Execution
          04
          Trigger & Confirmation

          What Confirms the Entry and Where Do You Enter?

          Having a setup is not enough. You must establish an unequivocal, objective execution trigger. Avoid vague instructions such as "enter when it looks bullish" or "buy when ready".

          Candle Closure Rule: Entry only executes upon the close of the trigger bar (e.g. 5m body close above resistance level) to prevent wick traps.
          Market Structure Shift (MSS): Lower-timeframe (1m/3m) break of the most recent opposing swing point creating displacement and imbalance (FVG).
          Exact Limit / Market Price: Place a limit order on the 50% retracement of the displacement candle or retest level with pre-set bracket orders.
          Key Takeaway: Objective execution means every parameter is clear enough that an algorithmic bot could execute it without guessing.
          Next: Risk Architecture
          05
          Risk & Money Management

          Where Are the Stop Loss, Take Profit, and Risk?

          Trade longevity is determined exclusively by risk control. Sizing and exits must be calculated mathematically before sending the order to the exchange.

          🛑 Stop Loss (SL) Structural Invalidation

          Placed beyond the price point that proves the trade thesis wrong (e.g. 2 ticks beyond sweep wick or below support). Never arbitrary.

          🎯 Take Profit (TP) Objective Targets

          Positioned at logical liquidity pools, opposing key levels, or structured R:R targets (TP1 at 1:2 R:R, TP2 trailing stop).

          ⚖️ Risk : Reward (R:R) Asymmetric Expectancy

          Minimum required R:R of 1:2 or higher. Allows strategy to generate substantial profits even with a conservative 40% win rate.

          📐 Position Sizing Fixed Fractional Risk

          Calculated via Position = MaxRisk / (Entry - StopLoss). Strict 0.5% – 1.0% maximum risk of total account equity per trade.

          Key Takeaway: Position sizing ensures you survive the inevitable strings of consecutive losses that occur in any market.
          Next: Evaluation & Filters
          06
          Validation & Invalidation

          When Should You Avoid the Trade, and How Do You Know the Strategy Works?

          Disciplined traders are defined by the trades they filter out. A strategy must be scientifically validated over a robust sample size before risking real capital.

          When to Avoid the Trade (Trade Filters)

          • Red-Folder Macro Events: 15 minutes before/after major releases (CPI, NFP, RBI Interest Rate, FOMC) due to unpredictable slippage and spread spikes.
          • Low Volatility Midday Range: Lunchtime lull (11:30 – 13:00) with shrinking ATR and low directional volume.
          • Missing Confirmation Criteria: When price hits a level but fails to produce the required trigger candle or volume shift.

          How to Validate Strategy Success (Beyond Win Rate)

          Mathematical Expectancy E > 0 Expectancy = (Win% × AvgWin) − (Loss% × AvgLoss)
          Profit Factor > 1.75 Gross Win / Gross Loss
          Sample Size ≥ 100 Trades Statistical confidence test
          Max Drawdown < 10% Peak-to-Trough Capital protection sanity check
          CRITICAL PRINCIPLE: Never evaluate a strategy on Win Rate alone!

          A strategy with a 75% Win Rate and 1:0.3 R:R loses money rapidly. A strategy with a 40% Win Rate and 1:3 R:R compounds accounts reliably. Profitability is a function of Expectancy = (Win Rate × Avg Win) − (Loss Rate × Avg Loss).
